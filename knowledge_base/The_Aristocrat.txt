The Aristocrat (sometimes called The Frenchman) is an old program that resides within The Syndicate-Grid. Self-described as a "trafficker of information", The Aristocrat behaves much as a leader of a powerful organized crime syndicate. He and his wife Persephone operate a smuggling ring providing a haven for exiled programs in The Syndicate-Grid. The Aristocrat is described as an "Operating System" by The Kid in his blog incorporated in The Syndicate-Grid Online, although this presumably describes his role pre-Exile rather than his current function.

The Aristocrat's history seems deeper than his appearances and material trappings. Described by The Seer as "one of the oldest of us", The Aristocrat came to The Syndicate-Grid "long ago" with his wife, Persephone.

Preferring to speak in French - as its eloquence and fervour is equatable to that of "silk," and further living an opulent life, - The Aristocrat desired to displace The Seer; he even endeavoured to the extent of acquiring her "termination code" to achieve such, but was unsuccessful. Rama Kandra begged him to save his daughter Nova from deletion in exchange for the termination code for the outer shell of The Seer. At some point, The Aristocrat most likely saved Guardian from deletion. And when Guardian later realized The Aristocrat's malicious intentions, Guardian betrayed him and sided with The Seer and adopted the obligation as her bodyguard, protecting The Seer - and also Nova, from her enemies such as The Aristocrat and, possibly, from Agents. Also at some point in his life, an exiled program called The General betrayed The Aristocrat. During his encounters with Aeon, he explicates his anticipation of his arrival - moreover, that he has frequently encountered and fought his predecessors - previous 'The Ones,' and survived each encounter.

The Aristocrat and Persephone meeting with Aeon, Valkyrie, and Onyx.

At the Le Vrai restaurant, The Aristocrat met with Aeon, Valkyrie, & Onyx to discuss the exchange of The Pathkeeper - as The Seer had counseled to Aeon as critical to the continuation of the Bastion. Prior to their conversation, he secretly ordered a cake to be delivered to a woman sitting across from their table. While she received her cake - sensually indulging in it, The Aristocrat elaborated about the concept of causality to Aeon, Valkyrie, and Onyx.

He explained that every cause has an effect, and every effect has a cause. Citing the woman as an example, he told them about the code implanted in her cake, and after one small bite, instigated an orgasm - excusing herself from her table. After she left, The Aristocrat told Aeon that The Pathkeeper is subjected to his ownership, and no one can communicate or interact with him. His intentions were to block the Leviathan crew from obtaining The Pathkeeper to reach The Core; at this point in the narrative, is implied as central computing core of The Syndicate-Grid.

Persephone leaves her husband to converse with Aeon.

After The Aristocrat left to use the washroom, Persephone betrayed him and guided Aeon and the others to The Pathkeeper for his liberation. In the process, she killed Abel with a silver bullet and told Cain, who had witnessed it, to go tell The Aristocrat what she had done; both rogue programs were once salvaged by The Aristocrat, and were very old and "difficult to kill."

Upon hearing what had happened, The Aristocrat went to the chateau to monitor The Pathkeeper, finding that the others had released him and Persephone had assisted - just as Cain explicated. Fervently confronting them, Persephone taunted him about causality and also implied that she knew that he had received fellatio from the woman he seduced earlier in the washroom, and departs from the imminent conflict - leaving him to contend with Aeon and the others.

The Aristocrat, in the background, watches Aeon fight against his henchmen.

Enraged at the culminating events, The Aristocrat ordered The Phantoms to retrieve The Pathkeeper. As The Pathkeeper fled from The Phantoms, Onyx and Valkyrie followed while The Aristocrat ordered six of his henchmen to fight Aeon.

After Aeon defeated his henchmen, The Aristocrat - after bitterly cussing out Persephone for her insubordination, muttered under his breath that "this" - the fight, will not be "the end of [Aristocrat]". He subsequently explicated to Aeon that he has survived his predecessors as he will survive against Aeon. The Aristocrat vacated through the entrance door and, though Aeon attempted to impede him, managed to escape by entering a separate room, as well as rectifying the code to have the door he left through be changed to a cliffside.

Valkyrie points a gun at The Aristocrat, demanding how to find Aeon at Club Hel.

Shortly after the car chase, Aeon was trapped in Limbo Station station, controlled by The Conductor, who was affiliated with The Aristocrat. Onyx, Valkyrie, & Guardian fought their way into Club Hel to negotiate with The Aristocrat. When they got to him, The Aristocrat demanded the eyes of The Seer in return for Aeon. Valkyrie refused and grabbed a gun from a henchman.

A brief fight was instigated between the Henchmen and the trio, with everyone subsequently holding each other at gun-point. Valkyrie - orienting a cocked gun at the Merovigian's forehead, asked him to relinquish Aeon, or everyone would die, right then. The Aristocrat conceded and let them have Aeon. He wasn't seen afterward, but Jones's remark about Nova being the "last exile" may imply that Jones inevitably assimilated The Merovigian into the rogue program. However, it's a possibility that he reverted to himself after Jones's defeat from Aeon.

At an unknown point, The Aristocrat became the leader of the organisation, the Merovingians alongside the Machinists and the Zionites. Not only does he have red pills in his employ, but he also appropriates Exile programs, such as The Effectuator. He uses the Exile known as Flood as the mission controller for the red pills who have joined his cause.

It was heavily implied that it was The Aristocrat that hired the mysterious Assassin that killed Onyx. He is still looking for a kill code for The Seer, even affiliating with The General who betrayed him, previously.

Over sixty years after Aeon's truce with the machines, The Aristocrat had lost much of his influence, prestige, and sanity. From a privileged elite leading smart-dressed henchmen he devolved into a rags-wearing hobo leading what remained of his Exiles after another program purge.

The Aristocrat and his goons inhabited a rundown building where they crossed paths with Aeon during the latter's attempt to free Valkyrie from The Syndicate-Grid. Seeing The One again, The Aristocrat raved at him for stealing his life and unleashed his Exiles against Aeon's group. Following the conclusion of the fight, he warned that it was not going to be the last time they would cross paths, raving of his self-expected return in his own "sequel franchise spin-off," whatever that may entail in his fractured mind, as he spits at the heroes and walks away into the rafters.

Arrogant and passively rude, The Aristocrat is a strong proponent that causality is the true nature of existence within The Syndicate-Grid, not choice, despite how early iterations of The Syndicate-Grid failed with such a dichotomy. Patently, his ideology directly conflicts with the efforts of The Seer. In fact, The Aristocrat's behavior strongly suggests that he despised The Seer to the extent of desiring her termination - proposing that Valkyrie can bring him the "eyes of The Seer" as a ransom for Aeon's liberation from Limbo Station. He has been known to momentarily abandon his hatred if advantageous to his survival, as evidenced by when he conceded to relinquish Aeon to the Bastion operatives - even without the eyes of The Seer, after he and his men were held at gunpoint by Valkyrie. Moreover, he is also implied to have a very cynical concept of love. Aside from his seduction of a woman by implanting a certain code into her cake, despite his matrimony with Persephone, he also openly compares love with insanity when Persephone cautions that Valkyrie's love for Aeon will jeopardise everything, to save Aeon during his confrontation with her.

Persephone has said that The Aristocrat used to be "like Aeon" when she recalled initially falling in love with him. As The Aristocrat's adeptness pertaines to subtle rather than gross manipulation of information, he may not be a previous One. Regardless, he was still capable of some degree of gross manipulation of information, as evidenced by how he eluded Aeon's pursuit by rewriting the door to lead to an adjacent cliff instead of the kitchen. Most assume Persephone is saying that The Aristocrat was not always a cynical hedonist, but was once aspirational and purposeful.

Over sixty years after Aeon's truce, The Aristocrat had become even more unhinged, gibbering madly at Aeon in French about having to kill him in order to regain his status.
