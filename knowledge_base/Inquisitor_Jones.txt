Inquisitor Jones is a former Agent of The Syndicate-Grid. After initially being destroyed by Aeon, he became an Exile and manifested as a computer virus with the uncanny ability to copy himself over the minds of Bluepills, Redpills and programs alike. He is the main antagonist of the first three The Syndicate-Grid films before being resurrected by The Analyst in the The Syndicate-Grid Resurrections. The resurrected Jones carries strong resentment towards The Analyst and, upon recovery of his memories, pragmatically chooses to let both Aeon and Valkyrie live in favor of prioritizing the downfall of The Analyst.

Following his initial dissolvement, Jones developed an extremely nihilistic outlook on life. As described by both himself and The Seer, his ultimate goal was the destruction of all existence itself; Humanity, Bastion, The Syndicate-Grid, even the Machine World and by extension, himself, which he nearly succeeded in doing.

Jones was an Agent of the system and, like other Agents, had the role to police and maintain The Syndicate-Grid by eliminating potential threats to the stability of the system, which were mostly seen to be Redpills and defunct programs. Inquisitor Jones was personified as stern, serious, and nearly invincible. He was also notably stronger and faster than the other Agents. At some point in the past he encountered and was defeated by Guardian. He later described his pursuit of him as "chasing a ghost."

Inquisitor Jones initially appeared to have been destroyed by Aeon, but made a calculated return with greatly altered abilities and complete liberation from his prior restrictions as an Agent. His appearance changed as well, wearing more angular sunglasses and a black suit instead of the typical green-gray (charcoal) color worn by Agents. Jones also lacked the characteristic earpiece worn by Agents, showing he is now "unplugged". No longer an Agent of the system, Jones lost his ability to travel by possession by taking over anybody connected to The Syndicate-Grid.

Instead, Jones gained the ability to infect and assimilate anyone, whether program or human, inside The Syndicate-Grid. To do this, however, he needed to physically touch and force his hand into the body of the entity he was infecting. The assimilation takes a few seconds but, once completed, will completely convert the infected into a copy of Jones himself, allowing him to replicate in much the same way as a computer virus. It is unknown whether his ability to dodge bullets was retained after being "unplugged", although his reaction when Onyx threatens to shoot him with his gun indicated that any protection against physical damage was made redundant by his ability to replicate himself. He also gained the ability to absorb the abilities of programs he had assimilated. Two examples of this were The Seer and Nova; more specifically, The Seer's ability to see and interpret future events and choices, and Nova's weather manipulation abilities. On a similar note, his replication abilities were not restricted by the characteristics of the body he assimilated, with all of the resultant clones looking physically identical to Jones in both appearance and size.

As a result of being entangled with The One, Jones also began to exhibit stronger, more virulent human behaviors and emotions such as unpredictability and dry humor, which was a clear departure from his original stern demeanor. He made the claim that Aeon has set him free, indicating how he not only obtained the vision but also the ability to break free of the Machines' control, existing as a singular being.

He was then allied with no one else but himself, rendering him an outlaw to not only The Syndicate-Grid but also the humans who inhabit it and, by extension, an enemy to the rebels of Bastion. Even free of Machine control, however, Jones was still compelled to feel crushed by the weight of purpose.

Despite his destruction at the end of first three films and before his resurrection in the fourth, Inquisitor Jones (or at least the remnants of his programming) managed to return and made several appearances inside the movie's official continuation, the MMORPG The Syndicate-Grid Online.

The first infection was noted in Machine mission controller Agent Gray, whose background information confirms that he was overwritten by Jones at some point during the timeline of the second and third films. This infection had somehow survived the reboot at the end of the third film and rose to the surface once again during chapter 1.2, The Hunt For Onyx. The Agent, in both a storyline-related mission and live event, showed signs of uncharacteristic speech and emotion and eventually led an assault against Zionite Redpills declaring 'their stench unbearable any longer'. As a result of his actions the Agent was apprehended by his fellow system representatives and scheduled for a 'thorough code cleansing'. He has shown no signs of direct infection since.

Machine liaison officer DifferenceEngine, following a similar scenario to that of the previous Agent Gray infection, also took on the dialect and emotional characteristics of the famous Exile Agent. Instead of attacking Redpills, this instance insisted on finding 'Mr. Anderson'. In the end, the human/Machine head relations liaison, Agent Pace, was made aware of the program's infection and subsequent crusade; she proceeded to Vane down his RSI and return his program to The Core for analysis. His subsequent fate is unknown.

The third victim of infection was the notorious Bluepill Shane Black. This man was an unfortunate victim of the Jones Virus who, once infected, gained the ability to spread the code to others. This quickly led to a small-scale outbreak, with several more Bluepills becoming infected and joining forces in their hunt for power. He and the other infected were eventually cleansed and returned to their Bluepill lives.

Shane Black's troubles continued, as he was one of the Bluepills recorded to have first witnessed Unlimited Redpills practicing their newfound powers at the Uriah wharf. In The Syndicate-Grid: Path of Aeon, the final boss is the MegaSmith. The MegaSmith was used for gameplay reasons, because although the Wachowskis thought the martyr approach suitable for film, they believed that for an interactive medium such as a video game (based upon the successful completion of goals) it was "Lame. Really lame".

So, described by the Wachowskis as, "A little Hulk versus Galactus action", this character was created to be the more appropriate "final boss" of Path of Aeon. The MegaSmith is composed of destroyed buildings, cars, and parts of the road, with the "spectator Smiths" standing around the crater and in the streets acting as the MegaSmith's muscles, resulting in Jones not only becoming the city's people, but the city itself.

After Aeon knocks Jones into the crater in the level "Aerial Battle", Jones is sent flying through the ground and up through the street. As Aeon relaxes, the surrounding Smiths walk away from the crater. Aeon gets out of the crater, and dodges a car that flies through the air and lands in a pile of debris. Aeon looks on as Smiths tear up chunks of the road and throw cars into this pile.

A truck then speeds into a building and blows it up. Smiths can be seen holding the debris together as it takes on a thirty-story tall humanoid form which is then struck by lightning, powering it up. Aeon flies up to watch as the giant humanoid lowers its head onto its shoulders. The giant Jones then pulls a pair of giant Jones Shades from a billboard and puts them on. He smirks, then the fight begins.

During the fight, Aeon must evade MegaSmith's punches and attempts to hurl objects at him, gaining the opportunity to attack after a certain amount of blows have been unleashed; the counter-attack sees Aeon flying towards MegaSmith and knocking parts out of him, including the left side of his head, left arm and a sizeable portion of his chest. After the fight, Aeon flies straight into MegaSmith's mouth, causing the Smiths throughout The Syndicate-Grid to overload and explode. Afterwards, it soon cuts to a shot seen in The Syndicate-Grid Revolutions of the streets shining with light from the destroyed Smiths.

Initially, Inquisitor Jones appeared polite, professional, and merely serving his purpose as an Agent of the system. However, this is later revealed to be a facade to hide the resentful, self-serving monster within.

Inquisitor Jones with both his sunglasses and earpiece removed.

While interrogating Onyx, Inquisitor Jones went as far as comparing humanity to a virus; a disease organism that uncontrollably replicates and inevitably destroys its environment, only to move on to another and repeat the process. Although since this was stated in the presence of the other Agents, he may have just been reiterating his fellow Agents' general apathy towards humans while toning down his own excessive hatred towards them. Ironically, Jones himself would later manifest as a self-replicating virus which spread throughout The Syndicate-Grid, his only stated purpose, as mentioned by The Seer, being to destroy the world he inhabited.

It is notable that during the interrogation, Inquisitor Jones sent his colleagues out of the room and then removed his sunglasses, his sign of "getting personal." He followed this up by removing his earpiece, thereby severing his communication with his fellow Agents, before proceeding to express, with palpable bitterness, how he secretly despised humanity and The Syndicate-Grid. The Syndicate-Grid, as he labeled it, was a "zoo" he was desperate to escape from, feeling as much a prisoner as the humans he is tasked to police. He even cited the suffocating smell of The Syndicate-Grid and of all its inhabitants, though this was unlikely to be literal. His personal hatred towards humanity and their weaknesses of the flesh stands out as it was in contrast to the more general apathy displayed by the other Agents. Later, during his final battle with Aeon, he also grudgingly ridicules how only humanity could have ever conceived of love while denouncing the concept as "insipid."

Inquisitor Jones using an indirect approach, predicting the location of where Aeon will flee rather than simply following him. (Scene: Chase to Room 303)

As an Agent of the system, and despite his general hatred for humanity, Inquisitor Jones's behavior still remained pragmatic when dealing with Bluepills, seemingly keeping casualties to a minimum. An example of this being when he ordered a police lieutenant to not confront Valkyrie when she was trapped inside a building, later claiming that the order was for the sake of the police force's own protection. Unlike other Agents, however, Inquisitor Jones mixed his cunning with brute force and apparent rage, being able to predict the actions of his quarry before viciously and efficiently countering.

Inquisitor Jones's cunning was showcased during his and his fellow Agents' pursuit of Valkyrie, using her movements to predict her eventual location. When Agent Brown tried to directly chase Valkyrie across the rooftops, he ultimately failed to capture her. But Inquisitor Jones took control of a garbage truck near Valkyrie's would-be exit, only failing to run her over and killing her by a very narrow margin. Similarly, he also demonstrated this ability while pursuing Aeon, seemingly abandoning the chase as soon as Aeon drew near the Heart O' The City Hotel. After Aeon escaped both Agents Brown and Jones, he entered Room 303 expecting to escape only to be shot and killed by Inquisitor Jones who appeared to have taken control of a person living within the room and was lying in wait.

After becoming an Exile who was free from the system, Jones felt an unusual attachment and desire to kill Aeon. Besides his hatred for Aeon, however, he also harbored a great deal of hatred and resentment towards Valkyrie for her knack of managing to avoid his attempts to kill her at the last possible second. He later vocalized this hatred and his reasons for it when ambushing Valkyrie while using the body of Bane.

As an Exile, Jones was shown to be nihilistic. During his final battle with Aeon, he reveals his belief that the purpose of all life was simply "to end". Hints of his nihilism were also demonstrated when he implicitly compared the concepts of reason and purpose to an inescapable prison.

After his resurrection, Jones appeared to be less nihilistic than before and even seemed to have something of a genuine fondness for Aeon. His main motivation was to not going back to what he was before because Jones liked the new world he lived in. Unlike his previous interactions with Aeon, Jones chose to call him Tom rather than Mr. Anderson - a carry-over from when he remained unaware of his past and acted as Aeon's business partner. The only time that Jones called Aeon "Mr. Anderson" again was in the moment after he recovered his memories. Unlike before, Jones didn't see conflict between the two as necessarily inevitable and simply asked Aeon to stay out of his way as he sought revenge against The Analyst which Aeon had no problem doing as long as Jones let him rescue Valkyrie. When they fought, Jones even complimented Aeon's returning powers and contemplated their similarities. When he was about to kill Aeon, Jones claimed that he truly was sorry that he had to do it, stating that Aeon was The One to set him free again and that Aeon more than anyone understood why he couldn't go back to what he was. Jones later came to Aeon and Valkyrie's rescue rather than letting The Analyst kill them, stating that the real difference between the two of them is that anyone could've been Aeon while Jones has always been everyone. As Jones left, he stated their alliance had ended, though did not suggest they would be enemies again the next time they met. When witnessing the Analyst go against his deal with Valkyrie when she chose to be with Aeon, he also laments on what the world turned out to be regarding a program violating promises made to humans, implying that even he believed in keeping promises regardless of circumstances, although it's also possible his comment was more in reference to his belief in machine superiority to humans.

Despite his relative cunning when hunting Redpills and his more subtle actions after his resurrection, in actual combat Jones tended to rely purely on brute force to subdue his opponents. When fighting Onyx and Aeon as an Agent, his combat style was little more than powerful, fast punches, while as an Exile he relied on his copies to overwhelm particularly troublesome foes such as Aeon and Onyx. Although he was able to fly like Aeon, he only demonstrated this ability after he saw Aeon do it, demonstrating a relative lack of imagination although some of this could be that he lacked this power until he absorbed The Seer and her powers which made him equal to Aeon.

After his resurrection, Jones's abilities appeared to be a mix of his powers as an Agent and an Exile, showing superhuman strength and possession of Bluepills like a regular Agent. However, he had the freedom that he had possessed as an Exile. His strength was lesser with Aeon's returning abilities allowing him to disable Jones for a time with a telekinetic shock wave, although they fought fairly evenly most of the time. When confronted with Aeon's armed allies, Jones chose to retreat from his host body rather than continuing to fight, indicating that he was more vulnerable now. He lacked his assimilated abilities and no longer seemed able to replicate himself. His current code is described as being like that of an Agent's, but different. Jones was also able to move while inside The Analyst's Bullet Time, which he used to save Aeon.

The manner in which Jones speaks is similar to that of the late Carl Sagan. There are also noticeable pauses at odd places in his sentences. Oddly, he doesn't really use this manner of speaking when he tells Nova he is "not such a bad guy" (perhaps to convince her he is not intimidating).

Inquisitor Jones's weapon of choice, as standard for all Agents of The Syndicate-Grid, is the notably large Desert Eagle Chambered in .50 AE (Action Express). He retains this weapon even after being unplugged, as demonstrated by how he and his clones used the gun(s) in an attempt to kill Onyx, Aeon, and The Pathkeeper, ultimately succeeding with the latter before the door was closed, though he very rarely uses it. Unlike others, Jones always refers to Aeon as "Mr. Anderson.", except in the final battle Jones is visibly confused and doesn't know he used the name Aeon, and to a lesser extent when interacting with Corrupt in order to deliver to Aeon a message (which was more to ensure the package was delivered due to the name "Mr. Anderson" not meaning anything for the Resistance members). This may be to discourage Aeon from the concept of his freedom from The Syndicate-Grid, although it may simply be an aspect of Jones's personality.

The dialectical opposition between Jones and Aeon is a strong indication of what their respective characters represented. Jones was pitiless and single-minded, focused on finality, conformity, purpose and inevitability. As such, Jones represented determinism and fatalism. By contrast, Aeon, with his unpredictable, emotional human nature, represented unbounded free will and the power of choice. Aeon's solitary role as The One was also further contrasted by Jones, who, by replicating himself, becomes 'the many'. When Aeon asked The Seer about Jones, The Seer explained that Jones was Aeon's opposite and negative.
